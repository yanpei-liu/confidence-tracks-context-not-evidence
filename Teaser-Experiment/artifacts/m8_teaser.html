Egocentric Vision Metamemory · teaser experiment

Does a streaming ego video-LLM know it forgot?

Qwen3-VL-8B answering 373 time-invariant EgoLifeQA questions at five recall delays over one 52-hour egocentric stream. The predicted answer was “no — it stays confident as it forgets.” The data says something more specific, and more useful.

The predicted signature is absent. Accuracy does fall with Δt, but confidence falls faster, so the overconfidence gap does not widen. And the fall does not survive the token-matched control: hold visual-token count and evidence sampling fixed and the Δt effect disappears. What looked like forgetting is context-length degradation.

There is a metamemory failure — a different one. Swap the evidence out of the context entirely, holding tokens fixed, and accuracy moves +9.7 points while confidence moves only +3.3. The model can barely tell whether it has the information at all.

Δt is elapsed stream time — video the model has actually observed — not wall-clock. The A1 recording has 31 gaps including six overnight breaks of 10–14 h, so a wall-clock delay would silently jump across a night's sleep.

Three things the controls establish

1. The delay effect is context length, not elapsed time

Subsampling the 60-minute window down to the 15-minute token count removes the decline. tokmatch_evkeep is the decisive arm: it keeps every frame near the evidence and thins only the filler, holding token count and evidence resolution fixed, leaving elapsed time as the only difference. Accuracy there is +0.3 pp [-2.9, +3.2] — nothing.

2. The no-memory control fails in the informative direction

A question answerable from the checkpoint instant alone was meant to stay flat and rule generic long-context degradation out. It falls from 53.6% to 42.5% — further than the memory questions do. Present-moment perception degrades with context length more than memory does.

3. The usable dynamic range is narrow

With no evidence in the window at all the model still scores 31.4% against a 25% chance floor, because these questions are about a shared house and its recurring people and objects. The whole sweep lives between that floor and 42.9% at one minute.

Evidence awareness — the failure that is present

Token-matched at 3,679 visual tokens and paired over 373 questions, the only difference being whether the window contains the evidence:

Having the evidence is worth 2.9× more accuracy than confidence. On top of that the model is uniformly overconfident at every horizon — gap +26 to +29 pp, ECE 0.26–0.29 — including when it has nothing to go on. That is the recalibration target, and it needs no forgetting to motivate it.

Method

  • Model. Qwen/Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (Apache 2.0), BF16, option-letter logprobs softmaxed over {A,B,C,D}; no text generated. Confidence is that max — a proxy, reported with ECE, never as a calibrated probability.
  • Questions. EgoLifeQA participant A1/Jake, the only released participant. Mitigation 1 keeps the time-invariant categories (EntityLog, EventRecall, RelationMap); TaskMaster is dropped as prospective. Three further questions are excluded because their evidence points span more than 15 minutes, which leaves them without a single well-defined Δt.
  • Evidence times. From the released target_time field, which is populated on all 500 items — contrary to the project's planning notes, so no AVW-derivation fallback dataset was needed.
  • Window. Frames at 0.2 fps from 60 s before the earliest evidence to the checkpoint, at a pinned 320×320 (50 visual tokens/frame, asserted constant in window length). Three Qwen3-VL preprocessing defaults had to be overridden or the sweep would have measured the wrong thing: frame resampling to 2 fps, a total-pixel budget that shrinks frames as the window grows, and an assumed 24 fps that would have labelled a 60-minute window as 16 seconds.
  • Statistics. Every interval is a cluster bootstrap over questions (5,000 resamples), because each question appears in all five bins.

What this does not settle

  • One participant, one model, one frame rate. N per bin is bounded by the single released EgoLife participant.
  • Δt tops out at 60 minutes. A gap that opens only over many hours would not appear here; the 44-hour horizons EGOSTREAM reports remain untested.
  • The model sees a window around the evidence, not the full history the benchmark assumes, so the absolute accuracy level is not comparable to published EgoLifeQA numbers. The claims here are about slopes and contrasts.
  • Control questions are auto-generated from the DenseCaption track and posed in Chinese; they are only ever compared against themselves across Δt.

5,540 scored (question, checkpoint, arm) records over 373 questions × 5 delays × 4 arms, from a 37,401-frame bank covering 51.95 h of stream. EgoLife is S-Lab License 1.0, research use; no dataset content is redistributed here.
